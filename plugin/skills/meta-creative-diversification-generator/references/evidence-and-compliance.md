# Evidence and compliance

Two jobs: say only what a public source or the account's own data carries, and keep every brief inside
the EU rules that apply to its cell. Cite sources by site and title.

## Evidence cards

No letter grades. Each card states the basis, the population, the window and comparison, whether the
effect is a bundle, one sentence you may use, and the forms you never use.

### Creative diversification

- **Source:** Meta for Business, "Demystifying Creative Diversification", 16 Dec 2025.
- **Basis:** Meta's own guidance to advertisers.
- **Population, window, comparison:** none stated for the guidance itself.
- **Bundle:** the post's percentages describe Meta's generative AI and Advantage+ features, not
  diversification. Never attach them to diversification.
- **You may say:** "Meta's guidance says ads that look or feel alike are treated as variations of the
  same creative and share learnings, and it recommends ads that differ in look, feel, storyline and
  message."
- **Never say:** a Meta number of creatives (the post argues against "a fixed volume of ad creatives per
  campaign"); that similar ads are penalised, blocked or hidden; any figure from the post as an expected
  result.

### The retrieval stage

- **Source:** Engineering at Meta, "Meta Andromeda: Supercharging Advantage+ automation with the
  next-gen personalized ads retrieval engine", 2 Dec 2024.
- **Basis:** Meta's engineering description of its ads system.
- **Population, window, comparison:** none; a system description, not a test.
- **You may say:** "Andromeda is the retrieval stage of Meta's ads system: it narrows tens of millions of
  candidate ads to a few thousand before the ranking stage."
- **Never say:** that the retrieval stage scores, prefers, rates or penalises creatives, or that it needs
  a certain number of them. Those claims are not in Meta's post.

### Fatigue and similarity

- **Sources:** Meta for Business, "Demystifying Creative Diversification", 16 Dec 2025; Meta Business Help
  Center, "About Account insights in Meta Ads Reporting"; Meta Business Help Center, "About creative
  fatigue recommendations in Meta Ads Manager".
- **Basis:** Meta's definitions and Ads Manager statuses.
- **You may say:** "Meta describes creative fatigue as an audience seeing the same creative too many
  times, and creative similarity as ad images or videos that look too alike. Ads Manager can show
  'Creative limited' or 'Creative fatigue' in the Delivery column."
- **Limits:** Meta says the similarity insight may not be available to every advertiser yet and covers
  static ads active in the last 28 days. This skill does not read either flag in this version.
- **Never say:** that the X-ray reproduces Meta's similarity or fatigue flags.

### Meta's creative diversity rating

- **Source:** Meta Business Help Center, "Creative diversity" (metric definition).
- **Basis:** Meta compares the images and video thumbnails in an ad set and turns the result into a
  High, Medium or Low label; Meta calls the metric "estimated and in development" and does not publish
  the thresholds.
- **In this skill:** not read in this version. If the user quotes it, show it as returned, unchanged.
- **Never say:** a threshold or score scale for it, or that the effective-creatives number is the same
  measure.

### Vertical video

- **Sources:** Meta Business Help Center, "Best practices for aspect ratios"; Meta for Business, "Reels
  ads"; Meta for Business, "Meta Advantage+ sales campaigns".
- **Basis:** Meta's recommendations. The product pages also show percentages from Meta's own analyses:
  some from split tests, some from large pools of ad sets, and each bundles vertical video with other
  choices (audio, text in the safe zone). None of them is a test on this account.
- **You may say:** "Meta recommends 9:16 vertical video for Reels and Stories placements, and audio on
  Reels."
- **Never say:** a percentage change in cost from adding vertical video.

### Incremental attribution

- **Source:** the values Meta returns when insights are requested with the incrementality attribution
  setting.
- **Basis:** Meta's own estimate, from its incremental attribution model, of the conversions its ads caused.
- **You may say:** "Incremental figures are Meta's own model estimate, returned by Meta; they are not a
  lift test run on this account."
- **Never say:** "true", "proven" or "measured" incrementality; never compare iROAS across accounts as
  if it were one scale.

### Account data

- **Basis:** this account's own spend, impressions and results in the stated window.
- **You may say:** "In this account over the last 30 days, the PRB cell reads ahead of its layer
  (index 1.20 on 30 results)."
- **Never say:** that the same will happen next month, in another account, or at a bigger budget.

## Never-say list

Do not write these, in any language, in replies, briefs, tables or client material. "N" stands for any
number.

**Numbers no cited source carries**
- "Meta recommends N creatives", or any other creative count per ad set or campaign presented as
  Meta's, a platform rule or a best practice.
- A similarity or diversity threshold presented as Meta's.
- A benchmark used as a target: "a good hook rate is N%".

**Platform myths**
- That the retrieval stage scores, prefers or rewards creatives, or "needs" variety.
- That Meta penalises, blocks or hides similar ads.
- That creative has replaced targeting, or is "the new targeting".
- That this skill reads Meta's creative diversity rating or similarity flags.

**Promises**
- "This will improve your ROAS / CPA / performance", "guaranteed", "proven to work".
- An expected uplift for a cell, a wave or a format.

**Verdicts**
- "Winner", "loser", "best ad", "worst ad", "kill it". Use "reads ahead of its layer", "reads behind
  its layer", "too early to read", with the result count.
- "Approved" for a cell or brief. The statuses are proposed, chosen, client (can, cannot, can
  differently), planned, launched, judged.

**Attribution**
- "True", "real", "proven" or "measured" incrementality.
- Adding attributed and incremental results together, or comparing them as if they were the same thing.

**People**
- Demographic labels for motivators or cells ("women 35-44 buy for…").
- Delivery by age or gender for an ad under a Special Ad Category.
- Copy that presents an actor or an AI-generated person as a customer.

**Invented facts**
- Any number, review, rating, discount or claim the user did not supply or the account did not return.

## EU claims register

**Not legal advice.** A checklist so the right question reaches the client's legal adviser before a
brief is produced. Add the matching rows to the brief's "Claims check" line.

| Cell or element | Rule for the brief | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| `VAL` with a price reduction | Show the prior price: the lowest price applied in at least the 30 days before the reduction; a percentage is calculated from that price. Member States may set exceptions (for example perishable goods, newly launched goods, progressive reductions) | Price Indication Directive 98/6/EC, Art. 6a (inserted by Directive (EU) 2019/2161) |
| Any environmental word (green, eco, sustainable, climate neutral) | Only specific, substantiated claims. A generic environmental claim needs recognised excellent environmental performance; a sustainability label needs a certification scheme or a public authority behind it; no claim that a product has a neutral, reduced or positive climate impact based on offsetting. Applies from 27 Sep 2026 | Directive (EU) 2024/825, Annex I points 2a, 4a, 4b, 4c of Directive 2005/29/EC |
| `PRF` (reviews, ratings) | Only genuine reviews. Say whether and how you check that reviews come from people who used or bought the product. Never submit or commission false reviews. House rule: no paid-for reviews in ads | Directive 2005/29/EC, Annex I points 23b and 23c, Art. 7(6) |
| `CRT` with an actor or an AI-generated person | Never present an actor or an AI-generated person as a customer.
